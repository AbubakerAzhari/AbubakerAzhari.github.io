01 — Overview

About the project

THE01 Rawasi Map (The Rsesidential Land Map Management System) is a Java-based desktop application developed to improve how residential land plots are visualized and tracked by the sales team.
Previously, the team relied on printed maps and physical markers to identify lands that had been sold or reserved. As transactions occurred, the map had to be manually updated, and new copies were often printed to keep track of changes.

This project introduces a digital approach to residential land management by providing a map-based interface for viewing land plots and their statuses in one place. It aims to make land information easier to access, map updates more convenient, and the sales team's daily workflow more organized and efficient.

The application was designed around the team's existing needs, with the goal of reducing dependence on printed maps and providing a clearer, more maintainable way to visualize land availability.

At a glance: A java-based desktop application desiged to modernize residential land visualization and sales tracking. The project addresses a traditional workflow in which sales representative used printed maps and physical markers to identify sold and reserved plots, requiring repeated manual updates and map prinitng.

02 — Problem statement

What was broken, and why it mattered

Before the development of this application, the residential land sales team relied on traditional printed maps to monitor the status of individual land plots. Sales representatives used physical markers to indicate which plots had been sold or reserved.
This manual process created several challenges:

  • 01

    Repeated printing

    Map updates often required printing new copies, resulting in recurring printing costs and paper waste.

  • 02

    Manual updates

    Sales staff had to mark plots by hand whenever their status changed.

  • 03

    Difficult maintenance

    Keeping printed maps current became increasingly inconvenient as transactions accumulated.

  • 04

    Risk of outdated information

    Different team members could refer to different printed versions, potentially causing confusion about land availability.

  • 05

    Limited accessibility

    Finding and understanding a plot's status depended on the available paper map and its markings.

  • 06

    Inefficient workflow

    Updating, replacing, and distributing printed maps consumed time that could otherwise be spent assisting customers.

The main problem was the lack of a dedicated digital system for visualizing residential plots and tracking their sales status in a convenient and organized manner.

03 — Objectives & solution

define, measure, then optimise

The primary objective of this project was to digitize the residential land map workflow and simplify the process of monitoring plot availability.

The specific objectives were to:

1 — Replace the repeated use of printed maps with a digital map interface.

2 — Make residential land plots easier to identify and visualize.

3 — Provide a clear way to distinguish plot statuses, such as available, reserved, and sold, where supported by the application.

4 — Simplify the process of updating land status information.

5 — Reduce reliance on paper-based tracking and manual markers.

6 — Improve the organization and accessibility of residential land information.

7 — Provide a foundation for future enhancements to land sales management.

Proposed Solution : The proposed solution is a Java desktop application that presents residential land plots through a digital map interface.
Instead of depending entirely on printed maps and physical markers, users can view the residential layout on a computer and identify plots through their visual representation. Where implemented, plot statuses can be displayed using distinct colors or symbols, making it easier for the sales team to distinguish between available, reserved, and sold lands.
By centralizing map visualization and the supported plot information in a digital interface, the application aims to simplify routine updates, improve clarity, and reduce the need to repeatedly print maps whenever changes occur.

08 — Challenges

What was hard, and what I learned

During the development process, important considerations included representing the residential layout digitally, making individual plots distinguishable, and designing a clear interface for sales-related use.
The project required translating a familiar paper-based workflow into a computer-based visualization experience. This involved considering how users identify plots, interpret their status, and access relevant information without relying on physical markings.

Map representation

Gluon Scene Builder was used to draw and simulate the company-approved map using shapes (like rectangle, polygon, lines) to draw the plot. boundaries, with each rectangle representing a plot.

Plot identification

Rectangles were used to draw the boundaries of the plots, with each rectangle representing one plot. Text was also used to display the plot numbers.

Status clarity

Colors were used to represent the land's status. Green represented green spaces, lime for all residential land available for sale, red for sold land and orange for commercial land. Different colors were also used to represent service areas (schools, malls, mosque).

Data consistency

Land status updates, land details and customer information are stored in MySQl / SQLite database. Reports are generated via databases queries.

Biggest takeaway: define your performance and accessibility budgets before you write UI code. Retrofitting them later costs several times more than building them in.

09 — Results & Benefits

Measured outcomes after launch

The project provides a digital alternative to the traditional paper-based approach to residential land visualization.

The main intended benefits include:

1- A more organized visual representation of residential land plots.

2- Easier identification of plot status through a digital interface.

3- Less dependence on repeatedly printing and manually marking maps.

4- A more convenient way to view land information during sales activities.

5- A foundation for extending the application with additional land management capabilities.

The extent of these benefits depends on the features implemented and how the application is used by the sales team. Further evaluation could measure changes in update time, printing frequency, and the accuracy of plot status information.
